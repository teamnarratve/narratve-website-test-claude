For years, the advantage in digital advertising came from targeting — knowing more about the audience than the competition did. That advantage is disappearing. Platforms now automate much of the targeting and bidding, and every advertiser has access to broadly the same tools.

When everyone can reach the same people, the difference comes from what you show them.

The ad is the targeting

Modern ad platforms learn from how people respond to your creative. A strong ad finds its audience efficiently because the right people stop, watch and act — and the algorithm follows. A weak ad struggles no matter how carefully the audience is set up.

That makes creative the biggest lever most performance campaigns have, and often the least managed one.

Why performance creative is often weak

Too often, performance creative is an afterthought: a resized version of a brand campaign, a product shot with a discount badge, or a template repeated until it stops working. The media team optimises bids and budgets while the ads themselves stay the same.

The result is familiar: rising costs, falling returns and a belief that “the platform has stopped working”.

Treat creative as a system

Better performance comes from treating creative as a system rather than a set of one-off ads:

  • Start with angles, not formats. Different reasons to buy — price, quality, convenience, status, trust — speak to different people. Test the reasons before the visuals.
  • Earn the first two seconds. In a feed, the opening frame does most of the work. It should be clear, human and relevant immediately.
  • Build for the platform. A Reel, a search ad and a YouTube pre-roll are different media. Make each one native to where it runs.
  • Refresh on a rhythm. Even the best ad wears out. Plan a steady flow of new variations before performance drops.
  • Learn in both directions. What wins in performance often reveals something useful for the brand — and a strong brand makes every performance ad work harder.

Creative and media, in the same room

The biggest improvement is often organisational. When the people making the ads and the people buying the media work from the same objective and review results together, the feedback loop gets shorter and the work gets better.

Performance marketing is not just a media problem. It is a creative one — and that is good news, because creative problems can be solved with better ideas.
