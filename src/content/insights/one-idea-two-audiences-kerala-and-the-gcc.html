For many brands in Kerala, the GCC is not a distant export market. It is family, history and a second home for a large part of their audience. That connection is an advantage — but it also means a campaign often has to work in two very different places at once.

Same brand, different context

A brand that feels familiar and established at home can be a newcomer in Dubai, Riyadh or Doha, competing with international names in a far more crowded media environment. The audience may be the same people, but the context around them is not. What they notice, what they trust and what they expect from a brand can change the moment they land.

Building for both starts with separating what must stay the same from what should adapt.

Keep the idea. Adapt the expression.

The strongest campaigns are built on an idea that is bigger than any single execution — a point of view about the category or the customer that holds true in both markets. That idea should not change. How it is expressed often should.

  • Language and tone. English, Malayalam and Arabic each carry different rhythms and references. Translation rarely works; adaptation by native writers does.
  • Cultural cues. Casting, settings, occasions and humour should feel natural to the audience watching, not transplanted.
  • Media habits. Where people spend attention differs between markets, so the channel plan should be built for each one rather than copied.
  • Regulation and approvals. Advertising standards and approval timelines vary across the region and need to be planned in from the start.

Diaspora is an audience, not an afterthought

The Malayali community in the Gulf is not simply an extension of the home market. It has its own routines, aspirations and moments that matter — festivals celebrated away from home, visits back, money sent to family. Brands that recognise those moments, rather than just repeating their domestic campaign, earn a different kind of attention.

Plan for travel from day one

The expensive mistake is creating a campaign for one market and trying to stretch it later. When the brief considers both audiences from the start, the shoot can capture what each market needs, the copy can be written for adaptation and the budget goes further.

Local understanding and global ambition are not opposites. The best work needs both.
